About the Centre & Iqbalian Epistemology

The Iqbal Study Centre (Iqbal Chorcha Kendra) is an independent, non-partisan intellectual sanctuary founded in Dhaka in 2023, dedicated to rational inquiry, youth emancipation, and civilizational renaissance.

Allama Dr. Sir Muhammad Iqbal

1877 (Sialkot) — 1938 (Lahore)

Cambridge University • Munich Doctorate • Lincoln's Inn

Intellectual Vanguard

A Synthesis of Eastern Spirituality and Western Epistemology

Allama Muhammad Iqbal (1877–1938) was one of the greatest philosophers, poets, and intellectual vanguards of the Muslim world in the twentieth century. Educated at Cambridge and the University of Munich, Iqbal was at once philosopher, jurist, political thinker, and poet — his creative output stands among the finest treasures of world literature, particularly Urdu and Persian.

Equally, his philosophy has opened a new frontier in contemporary international intellectual discourse; yet above all these distinctions, he was a bold and revolutionary voice against colonial servitude, psychological inertia, and a society in decline.

In today's post-colonial world, where Muslim nations — and Muslim youth in particular — suffer from acute identity crises and are battered by the assault of colonial cultural aggression, Allama Iqbal's liberal, progressive, and self-dignifying philosophy of life is immensely relevant.

Philosophical Pillars

The Anatomy of Iqbal's Thought

Selfhood

1. Khudi (Self-Realization)

The cornerstone of Iqbal's philosophy is 'Khudi' (Selfhood / Self-realization) — which teaches humanity to transcend inferiority and blind imitation (taqlid) and to awaken the dormant divine potential within.

"Elevate your Selfhood so high that before each decree of fate, God Himself asks you: What is your will?"

Community

2. Bekhudi (Selflessness / Collective)

Alongside self-realization, he articulated the theory of 'Bekhudi' (Selflessness / Community) — which motivates the individual towards collective responsibility and provides guidance for building a just, progressive, and equitable society.

"The individual exists in relation to the community; alone, he is nothing. The wave exists in the ocean; outside it, it is naught."
